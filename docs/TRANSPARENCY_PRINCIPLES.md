# Transparency Principles

## Personalise Access To Reasoning, Not Policy

The Institutional Reasoning Engine does not personalise institutional policy.
It personalises access to institutional reasoning.

An approved policy release remains common to everyone it governs. It is not
rewritten around an individual's circumstances, hidden behind a private ruleset,
or changed by the explanation interface. What becomes personal is the ability
to see how that common policy release applies to an authorised institutional
record at a particular point in time.

```text
Approved public policy release
  +
Authorised institutional record
  ->
Traceable personal view of the policy application
```

This is the product's central fairness boundary. A person should not receive a
different policy because the system knows more about them. They should receive
a clearer account of the policy that actually governed their case.

## The Four Questions

A subject-facing position view should answer four questions without creating a
new institutional decision:

1. **Where am I?** State the evaluated position in plain language.
2. **Why am I here?** Show the applicable conditions, recorded facts, and
   citations from the policy release.
3. **What process applies?** Distinguish a threshold, a human-review trigger,
   and a final institutional decision. Never present one as another.
4. **What can I do?** Provide the available route to inspect the record,
   request assistance, report missing or incorrect evidence, or request review.

The system must also be able to say that it cannot determine an answer. A
missing rule, incomplete evidence, ambiguous policy, or human discretion is a
visible limitation, not a gap to conceal with confident language.

## Non-Negotiable Boundaries

- The subject view reads an immutable reasoning trace; it cannot edit evidence,
  facts, policy, release applicability, or an institutional outcome.
- A human-review state is not shown as a denial, exclusion, or completed
  committee decision.
- Public guides remain separate from personal records. Personal views disclose
  only the authenticated subject's authorised trace.
- A review request challenges the recorded evidence, interpretation, process,
  or accessibility of an explanation. It does not silently alter the original
  trace or policy release.
- Subject-facing prose is rendered deterministically from trace fields and
  source citations. It cannot invent a requirement, citation, process,
  deadline, or outcome not recorded by the institution.

This preserves institutional consistency without treating opacity as a
necessary condition of institutional authority. It makes responsibility
actionable: people can see the rule that applied, the record used, and the
available next process.
